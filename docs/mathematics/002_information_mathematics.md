# Information Mathematics

## Purpose

This document defines the mathematical representation of information within Zoryn.

Information is the quantity that measures how much scientific understanding changes after incorporating new evidence.

The objective is not to maximize information.

The objective is to maximize meaningful scientific understanding.

---

## Mathematical Object

Let

S

represent the current scientific understanding of a system.

A newly observed fact

F

modifies this understanding.

The information contributed by the fact is defined as

I(F)

Information is therefore a function

I : Fact → ℝ

where larger values indicate greater scientific contribution.

---

## Information Gain

Suppose

S₀

represents understanding before observing a fact.

S₁

represents understanding afterwards.

Information Gain is

IG = Δ(S)

where

Δ

represents change in scientific understanding.

If

S₀ = S₁

then

IG = 0

No learning occurred.

---

## Properties

Information possesses

Magnitude

Direction

Quality

Novelty

Reliability

Information therefore becomes a vector rather than a single scalar.

---

## Positive Information

Information is positive when

uncertainty decreases,

confidence increases,

theories improve,

or explanations become stronger.

---

## Negative Information

Information may also decrease confidence.

Contradictory evidence is informative.

Therefore

negative information is scientifically valuable.

---

## Information Quality

The quality of information depends upon

measurement quality,

provenance,

independence,

reproducibility,

temporal consistency,

spatial consistency.

Information quality influences scientific learning.

---

## Information Accumulation

Information accumulates.

Multiple independent observations generally contribute more scientific understanding than repeated identical observations.

Accumulation is therefore nonlinear.

---

## Information Saturation

Repeated observations of identical behaviour gradually contribute less new information.

Scientific learning therefore exhibits diminishing returns.

Resources should preferentially observe poorly understood systems.

---

## Information and Novelty

Unexpected observations generally possess higher information content.

Novel behaviour therefore accelerates scientific learning.

Novelty becomes an important component of information.

---

## Information and Theory

Scientific theories increase the efficiency of information.

Strong theories require fewer observations to improve understanding.

Weak theories require more evidence.

---

## Design Principles

1. Information measures scientific learning.
2. Contradictory information is valuable.
3. Information accumulates.
4. Information saturates.
5. Novelty increases information.
6. Information quality matters.
7. Better theories improve information efficiency.
8. Information drives continual scientific discovery.
