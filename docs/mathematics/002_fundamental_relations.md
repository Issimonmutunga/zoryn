# Fundamental Relations

## Purpose

This document defines the fundamental mathematical relations between the primitive objects of the Zoryn mathematical universe.

Relations establish how mathematical objects are connected.

No computational behaviour is introduced.

---

# Definitions

## Definition 1 — Binary Relation

Let

A

and

B

be sets.

A binary relation

R

is a subset

R ⊆ A × B

---

## Definition 2 — Membership

If

x

belongs to

A,

write

x ∈ A

Otherwise,

x ∉ A

---

## Definition 3 — Equality

Two objects

x

and

y

are equal if

x = y

Equality is reflexive,

symmetric,

and transitive.

---

## Definition 4 — Identity

Every mathematical object possesses a unique identity.

Identity never changes.

Two distinct objects may possess identical attributes while remaining mathematically distinct.

---

# Primitive Relations

The following relations exist within the mathematical universe.

---

## System Membership

Every process belongs to at least one system.

belongs_to

⊆ P × S

---

## Process Participation

Every interaction connects two processes.

participates_in

⊆ I × (P × P)

---

## Observation Relation

Facts observe systems.

observes

⊆ F × S

---

## Evidence Relation

Evidence is derived from facts.

derived_from

⊆ E × F

---

## Support Relation

Evidence supports scientific theories.

supports

⊆ E × Θ

---

## Temporal Relation

Every object exists relative to time.

exists_at

⊆ (S ∪ P ∪ I ∪ F ∪ E ∪ Θ) × T

---

# Relation Properties

Every relation possesses

Domain

Codomain

Cardinality

Identity

Direction

These properties remain invariant.

---

# Composition

Suppose

R₁ ⊆ A × B

and

R₂ ⊆ B × C

Then

R₂ ∘ R₁

defines a composed relation

A → C

Composition preserves mathematical consistency.

---

# Inverse Relations

If

R ⊆ A × B

then

R⁻¹

is

R⁻¹ ⊆ B × A

Inverse relations exist only where mathematically valid.

---

# Cardinality

Relations may be

One-to-One

One-to-Many

Many-to-One

Many-to-Many

Cardinality is part of the mathematical definition.

---

# Mathematical Properties

Relations satisfy

Identity

Associativity of Composition

Closure under Composition

Well-defined Domain

Well-defined Codomain

---

# Corollary 1

Every scientific object participates in at least one mathematical relation.

No isolated mathematical object exists within the framework.

---

# Corollary 2

Every computational operator acts upon relations.

Operators never modify primitive objects directly.

---

# Computational Interpretation

Relations define the mathematical structure upon which future functions and operators operate.

Algorithms manipulate relations.

Relations organize objects.

Objects remain mathematically independent.

---
