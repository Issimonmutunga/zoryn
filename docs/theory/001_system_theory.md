# System Theory

## Purpose

This document defines the concept of a system within Zoryn.

Every observation, process, interaction and theory ultimately
describes one or more systems.

Systems are the primary objects of scientific inquiry.

---

# Philosophy

The Earth is not a collection of isolated objects.

It is a collection of interacting systems.

Understanding the Earth requires understanding

what systems exist,

how they behave,

how they interact,

and how they evolve through time.

---

# Definition

A system is a bounded portion of reality whose components interact
to produce coherent behaviour.

A system may be physical, biological, social, infrastructural,
computational or conceptual.

Systems may exist at any scale.

---

# Examples

Examples include

A watershed

A river

An aquifer

A forest

A city

An agricultural region

An electrical grid

A national economy

The atmosphere

The Earth System

---

# Hierarchy

Systems may contain subsystems.

Subsystems may themselves contain additional systems.

Examples

                                Earth System

                                    ↓

                              Climate System

                                    ↓

                            Hydrological System

                                    ↓

                                 Catchment

                                    ↓

                                River Reach

Hierarchy is recursive.

---

# Boundaries

Every system possesses boundaries.

Boundaries define

what belongs to the system,

what does not,

and how external systems interact with it.

Boundaries may evolve.

---

# State

A system possesses state.

State represents the condition of the system at a given time.

States may be observed or inferred.

States evolve continuously.

---

# Processes

Processes transform system states.

Processes may operate

within a system,

between systems,

or across multiple systems.

---

# Interactions

Systems interact.

Interactions transfer

matter,

energy,

information,

or influence.

Interactions create dependencies.

---

# Change

Change is the observable consequence of system evolution.

Processes generate change.

Facts observe change.

Evidence explains change.

---

# Scientific Perspective

Zoryn does not study isolated variables.

It studies interacting systems.

Variables become observations describing the behaviour of systems.

---

# Design Principles

1. Systems are the primary scientific object.
2. Every system possesses state.
3. Processes transform states.
4. Systems interact continuously.
5. Systems exist across scales.
6. Boundaries define systems.
7. Facts describe systems and their evolution.
8. Understanding emerges from interacting systems.
