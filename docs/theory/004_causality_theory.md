# Causality Theory

## Purpose

This document defines causality within Zoryn.

Causality explains why systems evolve.

Understanding causality enables explanation, forecasting, intervention and scientific discovery.

---

## Philosophy

The Earth is governed by interacting processes.

Processes do not merely occur together.

Some processes produce change.

Some processes influence change.

Some processes only appear related.

The objective of Zoryn is to distinguish between these possibilities.

---

## Definition

Causality is a relationship in which one or more processes contribute to the evolution of another process or system.

A causal relationship explains how change occurs.

It is stronger than correlation.

It is supported by evidence.

---

## Scientific Perspective

Observation alone does not establish causality.

Repeated evidence,

independent observations,

process consistency,

and explanatory power

increase confidence in causal relationships.

Every causal relationship remains revisable.

## Types of Relationships

Zoryn distinguishes between different scientific relationships.

### Causation

One process directly contributes to another.

Example

Rainfall contributes to river discharge.

---

### Influence

One process alters the behaviour of another without being its sole cause.

Example

Temperature influences evapotranspiration.

---

### Interaction

Multiple processes jointly produce change.

Example

Rainfall and soil properties jointly influence runoff.

---

### Dependency

One process cannot exist without another.

Example

River routing depends upon river flow.

---

### Feedback

A sequence of processes eventually influences an earlier process.

Feedback may be

positive,

negative,

or delayed.

---

### Correlation

Two processes evolve together.

Correlation alone does not imply causation.

Further evidence is required.

## Causal Networks

Causal relationships form directed networks.

                             Processes

                                 ↓

                            Interactions

                                 ↓

                            Consequences

                                 ↓

                            New Processes

These networks evolve continuously.

---

## Causal Evidence

Evidence strengthens causality when

multiple observations agree,

independent sources agree,

relationships remain consistent,

alternative explanations become less plausible.

Confidence grows gradually.

---

## Counterfactuals

Scientific understanding requires asking

"What would have happened if this process had not occurred?"

Counterfactual reasoning improves explanation and supports decision making.

---

## Intervention

Understanding causality enables intervention.

If a process can be influenced,

its downstream consequences may also change.

Intervention therefore supports forecasting and planning.

---

## Discovery

Unknown causal pathways are expected.

Failure to explain observed behaviour may indicate

missing processes,

missing interactions,

or incomplete theories.

Discovery therefore expands the causal network.

---

## Design Principles

1. Causality explains change.
2. Correlation alone is insufficient.
3. Causal relationships evolve through evidence.
4. Multiple processes may jointly produce outcomes.
5. Feedback is fundamental.
6. Counterfactual reasoning strengthens understanding.
7. Unknown causal pathways are expected.
8. Scientific understanding improves through continual causal discovery.
