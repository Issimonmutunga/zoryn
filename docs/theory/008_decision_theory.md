# Decision Theory

## Purpose

This document defines decision making within Zoryn.

The purpose of Zoryn is not merely to understand the Earth.

Its purpose is to support better decisions through continually improving scientific understanding.

---

## Philosophy

Decisions do not change the past.

They influence the future.

Good decisions are informed by

scientific understanding,

available evidence,

uncertainty,

and expected consequences.

Decision quality depends upon understanding rather than certainty.

---

## Definition

A decision is the deliberate selection of one or more actions intended to influence the future evolution of a system.

Decisions are hypotheses about intervention.

Their consequences must be evaluated continuously.

---

## Scientific Perspective

Every decision is made under uncertainty.

Complete information is rarely available.

The objective is therefore not perfect decisions.

The objective is scientifically justified decisions.

## Decision Evaluation

Decisions are evaluated by

scientific consistency,

expected impact,

risk reduction,

robustness,

adaptability,

and observed outcomes.

Evaluation continues after implementation.

---

## Decision Revision

Decisions are not permanent.

New evidence may require

modification,

replacement,

or withdrawal

of previous decisions.

Historical decisions remain preserved.

---

## Decision Feedback

Every decision produces consequences.

Observed consequences become new facts.

These facts improve

process models,

causal understanding,

forecasting,

and scientific theories.

Decision making therefore becomes part of continual learning.

---

## Ethical Decision Making

Scientific recommendations should

remain transparent,

remain explainable,

preserve uncertainty,

avoid unsupported conclusions,

and clearly distinguish evidence from assumptions.

Scientific integrity takes precedence over certainty.

---

## Design Principles

1. Decisions are evidence-driven.
2. Decisions acknowledge uncertainty.
3. Decisions remain explainable.
4. Decisions are continually evaluated.
5. Decision outcomes become new evidence.
6. Scientific integrity is preserved.
7. Better understanding improves decisions.
8. Decision making completes the learning cycle.
