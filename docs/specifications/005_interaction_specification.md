# Interaction Specification

## Purpose

This specification defines interactions within the Dynamic Process Network.

Interactions are the mechanisms through which processes influence one another.

Processes do not exist in isolation.

The Earth evolves through interaction.

---

# Philosophy

Processes produce change.

Interactions determine how change propagates.

Without interactions there is no emergence.

Without emergence there is no complex Earth behaviour.

---

# Definition

An interaction is a directional relationship through which one process influences another.

Interactions are inferred from evidence.

They are continuously refined.

---

# Canonical Structure

Every interaction contains

Interaction Identifier

Source Process

Target Process

Interaction Type

Interaction Strength

Interaction Delay

Interaction Persistence

Supporting Evidence

Confidence

Version

---

# Interaction Types

Examples include

Material Transfer

Energy Transfer

Information Transfer

Control

Constraint

Amplification

Suppression

Feedback

Competition

Cooperation

Interaction types are extensible.

---

# Direction

Every interaction possesses direction.

Source Process

↓

Target Process

Bidirectional interactions are represented as two independent interactions.

---

# Magnitude

Magnitude describes the influence of one process on another.

Magnitude evolves through time.

Magnitude may increase or decrease.

---

# Delay

Effects are not always immediate.

Every interaction may possess temporal delay.

Examples

Immediate

Hours

Days

Months

Years

Unknown

---

# Persistence

Interactions possess duration.

Some are transient.

Some persist for decades.

Persistence itself is observable.

---

# Evidence

Every interaction references supporting evidence.

Interactions without evidence remain hypotheses.

Evidence continuously modifies interaction confidence.

---

# Confidence

Confidence reflects current scientific support.

Confidence evolves.

Confidence never becomes absolute.

---

# Evolution

Interactions may

appear,

disappear,

strengthen,

weaken,

split,

merge,

reverse,

or stabilize.

History is preserved.

---

# Cascades

Interactions combine to form cascades.

A cascade represents sequential propagation through the Process Network.

Large impacts often originate from small cascades.

---

# Constraints

Every interaction

must connect two processes,

must possess evidence,

must possess confidence,

must possess temporal meaning,

must preserve provenance.

---

# Design Principles

1. Processes interact continuously.
2. Interactions are directional.
3. Evidence determines confidence.
4. Delays are fundamental.
5. Cascades emerge from interactions.
6. Interactions evolve.
7. Unknown interactions are expected.
8. Emergence results from interaction.
