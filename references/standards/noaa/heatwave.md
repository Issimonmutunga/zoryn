# Scope

This document defines the principles used by Zoryn to monitor prolonged
periods of abnormally high temperatures and associated environmental
stress.

Extreme heat is a cumulative hazard.

Persistence is generally more important than isolated temperature extremes.

# Core Principle

Heatwaves are characterised by persistent, unusually high temperatures
relative to the local climatology.

Duration is a defining characteristic.

---

# Pattern Characteristics

Heatwaves are typically

- persistent

- cumulative

- spatially coherent

- seasonal

Single hot days should not be interpreted as heatwaves.

# Planned Warning Logic

Heat warnings should consider

Temperature

+

Duration

+

Humidity

+

Night-time recovery

+

Population exposure

rather than temperature alone.

---

# Design Principles

Heat is cumulative.

Environmental reasoning should emphasise persistence rather than isolated
daily extremes.

---

# Limitations

Extreme heat impacts depend upon

- humidity

- acclimatisation

- urbanisation

- vegetation

- population vulnerability

Heat should therefore be interpreted together with additional indicators.
